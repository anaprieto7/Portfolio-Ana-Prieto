System context

WEMALO WMS is the core warehouse management system of the 4elements Group ecosystem, used by logistics operators and fulfillment providers managing inventory for multiple stores.

  • Master data (users, clients, products, warehouses).
  • Operational flows (inbound, picking, packing, relocation, returns).
  • Administrative management and reporting (services, movements, KPIs).

It is the backbone of logistics operations: connecting physical tasks (scanning, packing, transport) with the digital visibility that administrators and clients need.

Design challenge

The system was powerful and feature-rich, but its interface was outdated and visually inconsistent.

  • Dense, rigid design that was hard to scan.
  • Weak information hierarchy in complex modules.
  • Different visual patterns across sections.
  • Navigation friction between operations and configuration.

Constraint

Modernize the system without changing the existing technical architecture (Filters + DataTables), and improve the experience for both operational and admin users.

Redesign objectives

  • Improve visual clarity for dense data management.
  • Establish consistency across modules with a coherent visual system.
  • Lay a scalable foundation for future UI improvements without a full rebuild.
  • Adopt an enterprise approach inspired by Cloudscape (AWS), adapted to WEMALO’s brand identity (dark blue + orange).

My role & strategy

As the company’s sole UX/UI designer, I owned the entire visual redesign: from analysis to high-fidelity prototypes and design system decisions.

Instead of building a design system from scratch, I used Cloudscape Design System as structural base and adapted it to WEMALO.

  • Accelerated implementation with a mature enterprise foundation.
  • Maintained clarity and readability in dense interfaces.
  • Integrated WEMALO’s dark blue and orange to preserve visual DNA.

User research & discovery

Embedded research to understand real warehouse workflows and pain points.

Context & constraints

WEMALO WMS is a business-critical system used daily by warehouse operators, team leads and admin users. Due to operational constraints, research was embedded into the project through lightweight methods and continuous feedback loops.

Methods

  • Contextual interviews with operators, operations managers and admin users.
  • Workflow walkthroughs of Goods In, Goods Out and inventory-related tasks.
  • Baseline usability testing on legacy screens as diagnostic research.
  • Continuous feedback from stakeholders and developers during iterations.

Key insights

  • Cognitive overload: too much information at the same visual level.
  • Scanning inefficiency: users struggled to locate orders, status and actions fast.
  • Inconsistent patterns: filters and actions behaved differently across modules.
  • Experience dependency: senior users compensated UX gaps; onboarding was slow.

Core insight: the system had strong functionality — the main UX friction was visual hierarchy, consistency and guidance.

UX hypotheses

Clear assumptions that guided layout, patterns and design system decisions.

  • If dense screens are visually segmented into navigation, filters and content, then users will scan faster and make fewer errors.
  • If tables, filters and actions follow consistent patterns across modules, then users will rely less on memory and reduce cognitive load.
  • If hierarchy distinguishes primary vs. secondary information, then time-critical tasks will be completed faster.
  • If Cloudscape is adapted instead of reinventing components, then usability, accessibility and development speed improve together.
  • If the UI aligns with WEMALO’s brand while keeping neutral density principles, then trust and perceived system quality increase.

These hypotheses were validated through iterative prototyping, stakeholder walkthroughs and usability testing.

System analysis

Understanding the functional structure before changing visuals.

Functional groups

  • Stammdaten — master data for users, warehouses, clients and products.
  • Konfiguration — shipping profiles, packaging, data exchange and services.
  • WE (Goods In) — receiving, scanning, returns registration and quick entries.
  • Lagerverwaltung — relocations, inventory counts, refurbishment, stock state.
  • WA (Goods Out) — picking, packing, KO-box, transports, docks.
  • Berichte — movements, history, services, automated reports, serials.

Main conclusion

The system already covered the full logistics chain. The problem wasn’t functionality, it was visual design:

  • Too much information on flat, undifferentiated screens.
  • Little hierarchy between navigation, filters and content.
  • Inconsistent patterns across modules.

The redesign focused on hierarchy, consistency and visual scalability, without touching core logic.

Redesign process

From enterprise benchmarks to a scalable visual system.

1 · Benchmarking & references

I analyzed leading enterprise products for dense systems:

  • Cloudscape (AWS) — clarity and visual scalability.
  • SAP EWM — complex functional structures.
  • Odoo / Netsuite / Oracle WMS — modularity and hierarchies.

I chose Cloudscape as base for its readability and robustness, then adapted it to WEMALO’s brand.

2 · Visual language definition

I translated Cloudscape principles into a WEMALO-specific visual language:

  • Dark blue (trust) + orange (action) as core brand colors.
  • Neutral grays for background and table density.
  • Clear typographic hierarchy for titles and tables.
  • Separation between navigation, filters and content areas.

3 · Base component design

I built a Figma library of core components adapted to WMS needs:

  • Tables with sticky headers and hover states.
  • Tiered filters with active badges and saved views.
  • Contextual toolbars for repetitive actions.
  • Icons and indicators aligned with the color system.

4 · Prototyping & validation

I created high-fidelity prototypes for key modules (Inbound, Outbound, Inventory, Reports) and validated:

  • Technical compatibility with Bootstrap + DataTables.
  • Visual consistency across modules.
  • Documentation in Figma for future development.

♿ Accessibility with Cloudscape Design System

Leveraging AWS Cloudscape's built-in accessibility features to create an enterprise-ready interface that meets WCAG 2.1 AA standards from day one.

Why Cloudscape for accessibility

For a B2B warehouse management system used by diverse operators, accessibility isn't optional. Cloudscape provided:

  • Pre-tested components with built-in ARIA labels and keyboard navigation
  • Color contrast ratios that meet WCAG AA by default
  • Responsive focus management for complex data tables and forms
  • Screen reader compatibility with semantic HTML structure
  • Reduced implementation risk – no need to reinvent accessibility patterns

Strategic advantage: Using Cloudscape allowed us to focus on WEMALO-specific UX challenges rather than rebuilding foundational accessibility patterns from scratch.

Key accessibility implementations

🔤 Semantic HTML structure

All components use proper heading hierarchy, landmarks, and ARIA roles as per Cloudscape guidelines.

🎯 High contrast & color usage

Color palette follows WCAG 2.1 AA standards. No information conveyed by color alone.

⌨️ Full keyboard navigation

All interactive elements accessible via keyboard, with visible focus indicators.

📱 Responsive & adaptable

Components adapt to different screen sizes and zoom levels without losing functionality.

Cloudscape accessibility features we implemented

Data Grids & Tables

  • Row and column announcements for screen readers
  • Keyboard navigation with arrow keys
  • Sortable headers with proper ARIA labels

Forms & Inputs

  • Error states with descriptive text
  • Required field indicators beyond color
  • Field descriptions with aria-describedby

Navigation & Modals

  • Focus trapping in modals
  • Skip-to-content links
  • Breadcrumb navigation with ARIA landmarks

Status & Feedback

  • Toast notifications with live regions
  • Loading states with progress announcements
  • Status messages with appropriate ARIA roles

Usability testing & impact metrics

Lightweight testing used as both early research and post-redesign validation.

Testing approach

Phase 1 — Baseline (as research)

  • Tested legacy screens to identify friction and define a benchmark.
  • Focused on: finding orders, applying filters, understanding status & actions.

Phase 2 — Validation (redesign)

  • Tested high-fidelity Figma prototypes with the same user profiles.
  • Focused on speed, errors, clarity and confidence during tasks.

Due to the legacy environment, testing was iterative and embedded in the product workflow rather than a long formal research cycle.

Observed improvements

  • Faster task completion: quicker location of orders and critical actions.
  • Reduced cognitive load: screens felt calmer and easier to understand.
  • Fewer interaction errors: fewer misclicks and wrong filter/action usage.
  • Lower onboarding friction: less explanation needed for core tasks.

Estimated impact

  • −20–30% time to locate an order
  • −15–25% errors caused by wrong filters/actions
  • −25% onboarding time for new operators

Learnings & metrics

What worked, trade-offs and how I’d evaluate this type of redesign.

Learnings & retrospective

  • Adopting Cloudscape as base gave speed and consistency without building a DS from zero.
  • The biggest UX gains came from hierarchy: filters + badges + saved views in dense tables.
  • Brand alignment over an enterprise base elevated perception without sacrificing readability.

Trade-offs & collaboration

  • Less visual freedom in exchange for mature components.
  • Time invested in tuning density and spacing for WMS contexts.
  • Walkthroughs with operations + quick technical validation worked better than long testing cycles.
  • Figma documentation of components, tokens and usage simplified dev handoff.

Metrics I’d measure

Quantitative metrics weren’t tracked at baseline, but for similar projects I’d measure:

  • Time to locate an order (p95).
  • Clicks/scrolls to apply critical filters.
  • Errors from incorrect state selection.
  • Onboarding time for new operators.

Expected impact: −20–35% search time, −15–25% errors, and ~−25% onboarding time with better Saved Views and guidance.

Open questions & design ops

These questions emerged while redesigning a legacy enterprise system and would shape the next iteration

  • Which KPIs are critical for day-to-day vs. month-end reporting?
