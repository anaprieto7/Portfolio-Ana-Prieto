Handheld PWA
Mobile Warehouse Operations for WEMALO

Handheld PWA is a progressive web app for warehouse operators within the WEMALO ecosystem. The goal was to reduce time and errors in booking, picking and relocation — while preserving existing backend business rules.

Overview

Handheld PWA brings WEMALO warehouse operations to mobile devices. Instead of forcing operators to work from desktop UIs at fixed stations, the app lets them scan locations and products directly on the floor, with immediate visual and sound feedback.

My work spanned UX research, information architecture and UX/UI design — always respecting existing backend rules while reshaping the interaction into a touch-first, sequential experience.

Problem

Operational inefficiency and errors caused by manual sequences and desktop-only flows that weren’t suited for operators in motion.

Solution

A mobile experience centered on scanning with immediate feedback — guiding operators step by step without changing WEMALO’s backend logic.

Understanding the problem

From desktop-only flows to a touch-first experience aligned with WEMALO’s rules and warehouse reality.

Context

Before Handheld, operators depended on fixed stations or laptops with WEMALO’s desktop UI. This made repetitive tasks slow and error-prone in a physical environment.

  • Desktop UI was not usable on small screens or while moving between aisles.
  • Operators had to walk back and forth to complete simple processes.
  • Location and registration errors created traceability gaps.
  • Critical rules (like “scan location before product”) lived in the backend only.

We needed to bring WEMALO’s logic into a lightweight, sequential mobile experience optimized for scanning, motion and noise.

Research findings & design challenge

Early research revealed key inefficiencies and error sources in daily warehouse operations, shaping Handheld PWA’s design direction.

Key Insights

Early field research and shadowing sessions revealed structural inefficiencies in daily warehouse operations — directly shaping Handheld PWA’s design direction.

  • +4 km/day walked due to constant back-and-forth between aisles and desktop stations.
  • 70% of booking errors were caused by manual transcription and memory-dependent workflows.
  • Temporary workers required 2× more supervision during their first week due to unclear feedback loops.
  • Critical backend rules (e.g., “scan location before product”) were invisible in the UI, leading to repeated mistakes.
  • Operators trust visual + audio cues more than written instructions, especially under time pressure.

Problem clusters & design challenge

  • Fragmented tasks across multiple screens and redundant steps.
  • Frequent location errors from incorrect scanning sequences.
  • Physical constraints: desktop dependency and lack of mobility.

Design challenge: design a smooth mobile experience that respects WEMALO’s critical rules (like “scan location before product”) and optimizes daily operations without changing the backend.

Critical Desktop UI, not mobile
Main need Tactile, in-motion experience

Solution

Scanning-centered experience

  • Sequential scanning flow
    Each product requires scanning its location immediately before, mirroring WEMALO’s core business rule.
  • Immediate visual & sound feedback
    Green/red states, vibration and concise messages guide operators without long instructions.

The app enforces the correct sequence implicitly: users simply follow the scanner and colors, not a written manual.

Mobile architecture

I grouped the app by operational task type, starting from a simple dashboard:

  • Dashboard — entry point to all tasks.
  • Booking — register goods receipts.
  • Picking — unsorted and sorted picking flows.
  • Packing — prepare parcels for shipment.
  • Relocation — move products between locations.
  • Profile — user and device context.

Grouping by task type reduces search time and keeps the mental model consistent with real work.

Edge case: Booking without incoming order

During testing, operators revealed a frequent real-world scenario: goods often arrive without an existing incoming order in WEMALO. Previously, this required manual off-system workarounds that risked breaking traceability.

Usability testing

Validating the scanning sequence and feedback in real warehouse conditions.

Test objective

Confirm that operators understood the “scan location → scan product” rule through the interface, and that visual/sound feedback was enough to guide them without extra training.

Method

  • Participants: 5 operators (3 experienced, 2 new).
  • Duration: ~30 minutes per session.
  • Context: Real warehouse environment with mobile devices and scanners.

Tasks evaluated:

  • Register products using the Booking flow.
  • Complete a Picking order with 3 items.
  • Pack a box using the Packing flow.

Results

  • 💡 100% understood the correct scan sequence without verbal instructions.
  • ⚡ Time per scan dropped from 4.2s to 2.3s on average.
  • ✅ All participants recognized green as confirmation and red as error.
  • 📉 Sequence errors (“product before location”) dropped to 0.

Insights & conclusion

  • Visual and sound feedback replaced the need for written instructions.
  • Sequential flow reinforced the business rule without adding friction.
  • New operators adopted the system faster than with the desktop UI.
  • During testing, operators surfaced an operational scenario not captured in initial interviews: the need to book goods even when no incoming order exists in WEMALO. This exception happens in real warehouse practice and previously relied on manual workarounds.

From testing to design sprint: Booking without incoming order

Testing surfaced a high-impact operational edge case: operators frequently needed to book goods even when no incoming order existed in WEMALO. This scenario wasn’t verbalized during early interviews because it had become a normalized workaround inside the warehouse.

To address it properly — without breaking traceability or backend rules — we ran a focused design sprint with warehouse operators and WEMALO admins. The sprint explored how to:

  • Map the real workaround and identify where traceability was being lost.
  • Define the minimum required data to allow booking without an incoming order.
  • Align the new flow with existing backend validations and sequence rules.
  • Preserve scanning-first logic to keep the mental model consistent across all modules.

The resulting flow — “Booking without incoming order” — is included in Hi-fi flows → Booking. It formalizes a previously off-system process while keeping operators fast and the system reliable.

Outcomes

  • ~40% reduction in average time per task.
  • ↓ Walking distance per operator: –35% (from 300 stetps to 195 steps aprox for Testing Task)
  • Faster onboarding for new operators.
  • ↓ Supervisor interventions: –40% (from 1 to 2 interverts per task to 0 or 1)
  • WEMALO’s core business rule preserved with no backend changes.

Key challenge

Each product had to be tied to a just-scanned location, without slowing operators down. The flow resets context after each assignment and uses strong feedback to prevent mistakes.

“The system didn’t just need to be usable — it had to be fail-safe.”

Learnings

  • Design for reality, not theory.
    In operational contexts, every UI element must earn its place: prevent an error or save seconds.
  • Business rules as UX.
    Not everything should be “simplified”; sometimes the best experience enforces correct behavior.
  • Collaboration through logic.
    Understanding endpoints and validations helped propose solutions that fit the existing backend.
  • Tactile UX.
    Designing for gloves, movement and variable light changed how I think about interaction.
  • Think in ecosystems.
    Handheld is part of WEMALO, not a standalone app — continuity matters more than isolated screens.
